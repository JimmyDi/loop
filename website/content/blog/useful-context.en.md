Long tasks accumulate goals, inspected files, execution results and decisions that later change. Keeping everything does not guarantee that the next turn will find what matters.

## Preserve goals and constraints

Useful context starts with the current objective and the constraints that still apply. New requirements can change how work proceeds without replacing the original goal. A summary should preserve that relationship.

## Separate evidence from judgment

Tool results describe observations; judgment explains why a next step was chosen. Retaining the location of key evidence and its conclusion is often more useful than copying an entire output. Do not turn a planned check into a completed verification or a failed attempt into a success.

## Identify unfinished work

A useful handoff describes completed work, unresolved questions and what the next step needs to verify. Record specific reasons for blockers. When work is simply unfinished, describe it as remaining work.

The purpose of compaction is not merely to shorten history. It is to keep later decisions grounded in accurate facts.
